AI 2035

About the Project

AI 2035 examines how increasingly autonomous AI agents could scale cyber operations against enterprise networks and critical infrastructure, potentially outpacing defenders. It traces a capability trajectory from 2024 to 2031 before exploring two possible paths following a Taiwan Strait semiconductor shock: Plan P, where powerful open-weight cyber models proliferate, and Plan C, where frontier cyber capabilities are concentrated under state control.

To estimate potential economic consequences, I built a Monte Carlo model using 800,000 simulations, separating projected non-AI losses from additional AI-attributed losses across fraud, enterprise IT, and critical infrastructure. These are conditional model estimates, not observed losses or precise predictions. The project grew out of the AGI Strategy Course at BlueDot Impact, and I researched, wrote, designed, and built it independently.

About the Author

I want to work on questions around how advanced AI is developed, made safer, and governed. My technical background includes Python, C and Go, along with experience using Python libraries such as scikit-learn, NumPy and pandas, and working knowledge of machine-learning systems.

A lot of my work has involved research, verification and analytical writing. I have done independent verification work for EFF’s Atlas of Surveillance, checking claims against public records and documenting the evidence behind them. I am also interested in the philosophical questions raised by advanced AI, especially what would count as evidence for machine consciousness and how governance should respond when that evidence is uncertain.
