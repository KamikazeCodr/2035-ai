Plan P & C

Aditya Singh

Most public debate about AI risk focuses on ASI, future systems that are smarter than human experts at everything. In AI 2035, I examine an immediate crisis that could arrive much earlier. What happens when a single operator can deploy thousands of AI agents to find zero-day vulnerabilities, break into enterprise networks, and shut down power grids and water systems?

I examine a scenario in which, by 2030, offensive AI models can exploit the dependencies between telecommunications backbones, electrical grids, municipal water distribution, and maritime logistics, triggering cascading failures across sectors rather than attacking utilities in isolation. A 2031 blockade of Taiwan cuts off the world's supply of advanced chips, forcing superpowers to choose between secretly hardening their own grids while releasing open-source cyber weapons onto the internet under Plan P, or signing an emergency treaty that restricts frontier models as tightly as nuclear weapons under Plan C.
